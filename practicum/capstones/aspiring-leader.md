# Aspiring leader capstone: close a cross-team readiness cycle

Own the execution of one bounded incident, change, or NPI readiness cycle. Your audience is the service owner deciding whether the work is coordinated, whether the evidence is sufficient, and what risk remains. Keep your specialty as a technical anchor so your coordination reflects the actual failure mechanism.

## Make ownership testable

Responsibility names who performs work. Authority names what they may decide or change. Accountability names who ensures the result meets its acceptance condition. A task with an owner but no acceptance condition can remain ambiguously finished. A task with an acceptance condition but no authority can wait indefinitely for a decision no one owns.

Build a small dependency graph. For Meridian, current identity and telemetry evidence precede qualification; qualification precedes promotion; promotion precedes counting that asset as usable capacity. A vendor acknowledgement may precede a fix proposal but does not replace the qualification edge. Put each work item on this graph before assigning dates.

## Worked exemplar fragments

**Delegation, synthetic:** "Platform lead owns the isolated replay by the second review. Completion means the stale-generation event is rejected and a current-generation control is admitted, with transcripts. The lead may modify the test namespace and local fixture only; production policy changes remain outside scope. Escalate if the necessary current-state evidence is missing, rather than setting the check manually."

**Dependency decision:** "The capacity commitment remains at its last justified level until qualification evidence closes. I will ask product to choose between deferring eligible work and accepting a later launch; I will not count the repair pilot as installed capacity. The compatibility test requires six hours and a qualified reviewer, so I am reserving that dependency before assigning the remaining six engineering hours."

**Follow-through:** "At review two, the replay and control passed, closing the state-transition finding. Telemetry delivery remains open because only collection was checked. I returned that item with a new acceptance condition: demonstrate a missing-source event reaching the intended operator channel in the sandbox. This does not reopen unrelated passing work."

**Coaching observation:** "The delegate initially reported process running as recovery complete. I asked them to trace the user-visible postcondition and identify a counterexample. They added a restore correctness check and explained why it was necessary." This can support a course-observed coaching interaction if observed. It cannot establish sustained development of a team member.

## Run two real course cycles

At the first session, assign two bounded tasks to peers or explicit simulated role holders. Ask each to restate the expected result and escalation boundary. Record changes prompted by their feedback. Between sessions, complete at least one actual local course task yourself or with a participant; scripted claims that someone completed it are not evidence.

At the second session, accept, reject, or return each result against the original criteria. Respond to the instructor's new constraint. Update dates, owners, and residual risk. Close one finding with a demonstrated check; retain another as open if evidence is insufficient. Communicate the decision in 150 words using impact, evidence, action, and next checkpoint.

Final artifact: a dependency/ownership map, two decision versions, acceptance records, completed technical evidence, observed delegation notes, and a handoff. Do not submit a calendar without results.

## Assessment and remediation

The reviewer scores clarity of authority, dependency-aware sequencing, technical challenge, response to new evidence, and closure. Delegation passes when someone understood and used the boundary, not just when their name appears in a table. Leadership remains course-observed and role-limited. A solo learner may practice, but independent Lead assessment needs an observer or peer review.

If follow-through is missing, run another bounded work cycle and review actual results. If the decision ignores a technical dependency, trace the causal path before replanning. Do not repair weak evidence with more polished status language. No external stakeholder messages are sent by this exercise.
