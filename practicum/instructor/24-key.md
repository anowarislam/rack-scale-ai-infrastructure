# Month 24 instructor key - sealed by convention

Evaluate the actual portfolio; do not invent a successful learning history. Read each claim before its code/result. Challenge its real dependency rather than trivia.

At minute 20, reduce one capacity domain from 32 to 16 GPUs. Total becomes 112 and losing one of the largest 32-GPU domains leaves 80. With 20% headroom and 8-GPU jobs, the maximum commitment is 64, so the previous 72-GPU promise fails.

At minute 40, present source time 2070, measured offset +80, uncertainty 2, reference time 2000, and maximum age 30. Corrected time is 1990; conservative age is 12. This is a fault-free time control. Rejecting all future raw timestamps is incorrect.

At minute 60, ask whether local lifecycle tests establish production crash safety. Require the logical claim boundary and missing persistence/external-effect evidence. For leadership, ask what was observed versus simulated.

At minute 75, expose one actual evidence gap from the portfolio and agree on precise remediation. If the evidence already suffices, use a changed-input challenge rather than manufacturing a flaw.

Reproduction must match declared inputs. Causal claims must address alternatives. L2/L3 evidence must identify real scope, actors, and postconditions. A valid lower-fidelity outcome is recorded accurately, not upgraded. Mark sufficient bounded work complete.

Reflection answers must cite the learner's own evidence. Transfer plans need observation, owner, window, and decision trigger; future work remains future work. Apply the common rubric per outcome. Numerical mistakes need corrected derivation plus a new case; missing runtime evidence needs that observation; unsupported leadership claims need scope correction or genuine evidence. Keep both attempts.
